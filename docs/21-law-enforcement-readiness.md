# 21. Law enforcement readiness: US and Canada

**For a police service, a prosecutor's office or another agency that wants
to run NocTORnal on real casework in the United States or Canada.** This is
the order of work between "it installs" and "an exhibit from it can go to
court".

Nothing here is legal advice. Every determination below belongs to the
agency and its counsel, and the agency answers for them. The software
supplies the technical primitives; the agency supplies the legal authority,
the hardened deployment and the written process. Until sections 1 to 4 are
done, load synthetic data or published reporting with no personal data
only.

---

## What the evidence handling is built on

The evidence handling is architected on two sets of rules (decision 13 in
[docs/00](00-decisions.md)):

* **US Federal Rules of Evidence 902(13) and 902(14).** 902(13)
  self-authenticates a record generated by an electronic process or system
  that produces an accurate result. 902(14) self-authenticates data copied
  from a device, storage medium or file and authenticated by a process of
  digital identification, which in practice is a hash comparison. Both need
  a certification by a qualified person that meets Rule 902(11) or (12),
  and the reasonable written notice to the adverse party that 902(11)
  requires.
* **Canada Evidence Act, sections 31.1 to 31.8.** Section 31.1 puts the
  burden of proving an electronic document authentic on whoever offers it.
  Sections 31.2 and 31.3 satisfy the best evidence rule by proof of the
  integrity of the electronic documents system, presumed when the system
  was operating properly at all material times. Section 31.5 admits
  evidence of a recordkeeping standard or practice, and section 31.6 lets
  those matters be proven by affidavit, with the deponent open to
  cross-examination.

What each rule asks for, what the build supplies, and what only the agency
can supply:

| Rule | Asks for | The build supplies | Only the agency supplies |
|---|---|---|---|
| FRE 902(14) | data identified by a digital process, such as a hash | SHA-256 and BLAKE3 of every exhibit at lodging; the bytes re-verified against them on every read and before every export, refusing on a mismatch; both hashes in every report's evidence register | the qualified person who certifies the comparison, and the 902(11) notice |
| FRE 902(13) | a process or system that produces an accurate result | the [five-part access gate](../README.md#the-access-gate), the [twelve invariants](../README.md#the-twelve-invariants) enforced by database triggers, and a hash-chained audit log and custody ledger that `GET /api/v1/audit/verify` and `GET /api/v1/audit/custody/verify` recompute | the certification, and the record of how this deployment was configured, changed and checked |
| CEA s. 31.1 | evidence that the document is what it purports to be | each exhibit's custody from its `ACQUIRED` row on, with every `VIEWED`, `EXPORTED`, `HASH_VERIFIED` and `LOCK_EXTENDED` after it | the witness who explains it |
| CEA ss. 31.2, 31.3 | the integrity of the electronic documents system at all material times | write-once storage (a COMPLIANCE object lock on every exhibit, proven by the readiness register trying to delete a locked canary and being refused), and a database role for the API that cannot disable the triggers | proof the system ran properly at all material times: monitoring, change control, and scheduled chain checks anchored off the host |
| CEA s. 31.5 | a standard, procedure, usage or practice | the mechanisms documented in this repository | written procedures, measured against a recordkeeping standard such as CAN/CGSB-72.34-2024, *Electronic records as documentary evidence* |
| CEA s. 31.6 | an affidavit, open to cross-examination | the custody log, the verification output and the report for each exhibit | the deponent |

Two limits hold on both sides of the border. These rules settle
authenticity and best evidence only: hearsay, relevance, confrontation and
Charter objections are untouched (the 2017 committee note to FRE 902;
CEA s. 31.7). And a design target is not a ruling: the build is unaudited
and not certified for evidential use ([Status](../README.md#status)), and
whether a certification in this form satisfies a given court is
[docs/16 C1](16-legal-and-external.md#c1-evidence-authenticity-standards),
a question for counsel.

## 1. Legal review and written determinations

This is the gate. Nothing below runs on real material until it is closed.

* Give counsel [docs/18](18-legal-review-pack.md), the review pack, and
  [docs/16](16-legal-and-external.md), the register behind it. Answer every
  Section A question in writing in docs/18's Determination rows, dated and
  initialled, so nobody re-makes them under pressure.
* Confirm a lawful basis for each category of data the agency will load,
  stealer logs, breach data and the personal data of uninvolved victims
  first (docs/16 L2).
* Write the prohibited-content policy for malware samples, where possession
  alone can be an offence: preserve or destroy rejected samples, and on
  what authority (docs/16 L1). Preserving is the default;
  `NOCTORNAL_REJECTED_SAMPLE_DISPOSITION=destroy` is the opt-in. Then set
  `NOCTORNAL_PROHIBITED_CONTENT_POLICY` and `NOCTORNAL_DESIGNATED_PERSON` to
  the policy's reference and the person who answers for it. Sample ingest
  refuses until both are set, and the API does not start on the template's
  placeholders.
* Record the authority for persona operation, passive polling, message
  capture and any active web interaction, one legal basis for each
  (docs/16 L3, L4, L5). The schema refuses to record a submission into a
  phishing page without a written authority reference.
* Set retention periods, legal-hold rules, the disclosure procedure and any
  victim-notification duty. The seeded 90 days is a placeholder
  (docs/16 D3): confirm each rule under Records, Retention, and the blocking
  readiness check `retention_rules_confirmed` turns green.
* Check the intended use against interception, computer-misuse, privacy
  and evidence law. The usual starting points for counsel:

| | United States | Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Authenticity | FRE 901, 902(11), 902(13), 902(14); the state's evidence code in state court | Canada Evidence Act ss. 31.1 to 31.8 in criminal proceedings; the provincial Evidence Act in provincial matters |
| Interception and stored communications | Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. 2510 to 2523), Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. 2701 to 2713), state one-party and all-party consent laws | Criminal Code Part VI; warrants and production orders under Part XV; Charter s. 8 |
| Computer misuse | Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (18 U.S.C. 1030) and state equivalents | Criminal Code s. 342.1 and s. 430(1.1) |
| Disclosure | Brady and Giglio; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 16 | R. v. Stinchcombe, [1991] 3 SCR 326 |
| Privacy and data handling | Privacy Act of 1974 for a federal agency, state law otherwise; the FBI CJIS Security Policy for any criminal justice information | Privacy Act for a federal institution, the provincial or municipal privacy law otherwise |

* Record, with the determinations, that nothing in this repository is legal
  advice and that the agency accepts responsibility for them.

## 2. Written procedures

* Operating procedures mapped to the five-part access gate: which roles
  grant which verbs, who is assigned to which case, clearance by TLP,
  compartments, and when step-up MFA is asked for
  ([docs/05](05-security-rbac.md)).
* Two-person control where the build has it, and a decision where it is
  optional. Releasing a rejected sample takes a Security Officer and one
  named Lead investigator. Break-glass is invoked by a `CASE_OWNER` or
  `SYS_ADMIN` and reviewed by a `SECURITY_OFFICER`, and no role may hold both
  (docs/16 D7). Dual control on merge ships off (docs/16 D1).
* TLP handling and egress: every outbound path asks one `can_egress`, and in
  production the egress proxy is the only way out
  ([docs/20](20-outbound-connections.md)). Write down who approves a
  destination.
* Legal holds, the retention sweep (`scripts/retention_sweep.py`, a dry run
  first, then `--apply`), and the dead-letter queue, which can hold victim
  credentials (docs/18 D1).
* Who grades an assertion, who accepts a machine proposal, and who retracts
  or supersedes one. Nothing is overwritten: a retraction is a stamp on the
  row and a correction is a new assertion.
* The chain-of-custody procedure as it will actually run: lodging, hash
  verification, custody ledger review, export for disclosure.

## 3. Production deployment

Follow [infra/production/README.md](../infra/production/README.md). The
development stack and [QUICKSTART.md](../QUICKSTART.md) are not hardened
for real material.

* Write the secrets files with the production step, never the examples as
  they stand. The API refuses to start on a published or placeholder
  credential and names each variable it refuses. `secrets.env` is plain
  text on disk, so it belongs under the agency's own secret-management
  controls.
* Run the API as `noctornal_app`, which does not own the schema and cannot
  `ALTER TABLE ... DISABLE TRIGGER`, so the audit and custody chains cannot
  be written around from the API. Production refuses to start as the owner
  or a superuser.
* Confirm write-once storage. Every exhibit is written under its own
  COMPLIANCE object lock; the readiness checks
  `evidence_bucket_object_lock` and `preservation_bucket_object_lock` must
  be green. Weigh [docs/16 C2](16-legal-and-external.md#c2-minio-compliance-object-lock):
  the pinned MinIO build is archived upstream, and a COMPLIANCE lock cannot
  be shortened even for a deletion order.
* Run the collector as its own service. It alone holds
  `NOCTORNAL_PERSONA_KEK`, so the API process cannot open a persona
  credential. That is a process boundary, not a network zone.
* Two DNS names, one for the console and one for sample downloads. Caddy
  terminates TLS, 1.3 only, and 80 and 443 are the only published ports. Confirm
  from the host that the internal network has no route out, as the
  production README's egress section shows.
* Back up the database, the evidence and raw buckets and the env files
  together, encrypted before they reach a disk, and test a restore. Nothing
  in the stack does this for you.
* Read the readiness register (`GET /api/v1/admin/readiness`): the four
  blocking checks green and every other red understood. A green register
  says the code-side preconditions hold; it does not say the deployment is
  lawful.
* Test row-level security, TLP gating and the access gate in the production
  configuration, with the agency's own accounts.

## 4. The evidential foundation

* Commission an independent review of the custody mechanisms: write-once
  storage, dual hashing with re-verification on read, the append-only
  custody ledger with reads in it, and the assertion triggers.
* Write the process record a certification or an affidavit will rest on:
  who lodges, how hashes are checked, who can reach what, and how the
  ledger is preserved. In a US court it supports the 902(13) and 902(14)
  certification; in a Canadian court, the s. 31.3 integrity evidence and
  the s. 31.5 standard.
* Assemble the expert foundation: [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md),
  [docs/02](02-architecture.md), the twelve invariants and the triggers
  that enforce them, CI results, the deployment's configuration and its
  change records.
* Show the invariants hold on the agency's own deployment under realistic
  load, concurrent access and failure, such as the object store going away
  mid-ingest or a restart during a write.
* Anchor the chains off the host. Schedule
  `python scripts/audit_verify.py --anchor-file <file> --record` from a
  machine the database host cannot write. Without an anchor a check cannot
  see the newest rows cut off, or the chain rewritten and re-hashed by the
  schema owner ([docs/05](05-security-rbac.md)).
* Know how to produce, for any exhibit: its custody log
  (`GET /api/v1/cases/{case_id}/evidence/{evidence_id}/custody`), a fresh
  verification (`POST /api/v1/cases/{case_id}/evidence/{evidence_id}/verify`),
  the chain checks with their anchors, and the assertions that cite it.

## 5. Operational readiness

* Train analysts, investigators and administrators on the assertion model
  (nothing is a fact), the custody ledger, TLP, and the difference between
  a proposal and an accepted assertion. The
  [manual](../release/MANUAL.md) is the operator's reference.
* Assign the roles, at least one `SECURITY_OFFICER` among them: break-glass
  refuses without one, and `security_officer_present` is a blocking check.
* Run the whole lifecycle on synthetic data first: ingest, assertion,
  custody, legal hold, export, verification. The bundled showcase case is
  TLP:CLEAR fiction for exactly that.
* Add monitoring. A failed integrity check raises an urgent
  `EVIDENCE_INTEGRITY_ALARM` notification; beyond the notifications the
  stack has no metrics, alerting or log aggregation. Alert on integrity
  failures, authorisation denials and failed chain checks, and read the
  readiness register on a schedule.
* Write an incident and break-glass procedure that keeps the audit trail
  intact.
* Set a review date for the legal determinations, retention and the
  deployment configuration.

## 6. Ongoing governance

* Keep every legal determination and policy change on record.
* Re-validate after each upgrade or configuration change: the readiness
  register, the anchored chain checks and a synthetic lifecycle run.
  [release/CHANGELOG.md](../release/CHANGELOG.md) has the upgrade steps for
  each release.
* Keep real material inside the written determinations.
* Plan disclosure: how the assertion chain, the write-once exhibit and the
  custody ledger will be produced and explained, under Brady and Rule 16 in
  the US and Stinchcombe in Canada.

Completing these is what this project states is required before real
material is loaded. The software supplies the mechanisms; the agency
supplies the authority, the hardened deployment and the process.
